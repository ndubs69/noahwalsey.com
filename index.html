Certificate request · a process comparison, not a time study

The manual way

  1. The request lands in a shared inbox.
  2. Someone notices it between calls.
  3. They look up the policy and the holder.
  4. They fill in the certificate and type a reply.
  5. No one tracks whether it went out.

With an assistant

  1. The request lands and gets flagged right away.
  2. It pulls the insured and holder details from the email.
  3. It drafts the certificate request and the reply.
  4. Staff reviews and taps approve.
  5. It’s logged on the tracker as done.

Questions business owners ask

FAQ, straight answers.

Will it email my clients without my OK?

Only if you choose. You pick: approve every email, or let routine ones like reminders, confirmations and newsletters send on their own. First-touch outreach, claims and anything about money or coverage stay approval-first by default. The demos on this page never send anything.

Do I have to switch my agency management system?

No. I work around the system you already use. I confirm what access it allows before I build, and if it limits access, I tell you up front.

Where does my client data live?

In your accounts. Each assistant only gets the access its one job needs, and I never put phone numbers or emails on public demos.

What if it makes a mistake?

Drafts get reviewed before they go out, scheduled checks confirm each job ran, and anything the data can’t support shows as “unknown” instead of a guess. The pilot runs read-only beside your team before it goes live.

Why work with you instead of a bigger agency?

You work with the person who builds it. I run a team of AI assistants on my own work every day under these same rules, I know insurance operations from the inside, and I take one workflow at a time so it ships right instead of sitting in a backlog.

What does the scan cost?

Nothing. The 20-minute scan is free. If a pilot makes sense, it’s a fixed fee quoted after the scan. If AI isn’t the answer, I’ll say so.
